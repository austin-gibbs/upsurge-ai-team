# The AI Team Bundle: What Comes With the Service

Version 1.0, 2026-11-04. Owner: Nova (service-innovation) with Wes (web-dev-lead), Muse lane, for UpSurge Pros.
Status: the inclusion architecture for the AI Team service. This document defines everything a client receives, what they do with it, and why each piece is hard to copy. Companion documents: the Install System master document (architecture and install stages), the sales-to-install ops bridge (oversight mechanics and the weekly fleet report), the heartbeat page spec (the team page build), and the Team Pack document (roster, routing, QA checklists).

## The design principle

Nothing in this bundle is a feature list. Every item is included because it does one of three jobs: it removes operational work from the owner, it proves the work is getting done, or it makes the team smarter about that client's business over time. The bundle itself is the competitive edge: a competitor can rent the same AI models we use, but they cannot hand a client an installed organization with a working board, a living memory, a named roster with quality bars, and an oversight firm standing behind it. Assembly is the moat, and every item below names its mechanism.

## Group A: The Command App suite

The Surge Command app (the Desk) is the client's private command center and the board of record for all work. It ships in every tier. Five components:

**A1. Project board.** What it is: every request the client has ever made, as a project with a status, an owner, and a result, in Board, List, and Table views. What the client does with it: checks status in ten seconds instead of sending a "just checking in" message, reviews anything waiting on their decision in one place, and searches history when a question comes back months later. Why it is hard to copy: the board is not a generic project tool with our logo on it. Every project carries an execution brief written by the team, a lane, a named specialist, and a client-facing summary, and the status discipline behind it (nothing sits in a vague state, blockers are named in plain words) is an operating habit enforced by the Team Pack, not a software setting.

**A2. Team heartbeat page.** What it is: the /team page specified in the heartbeat spec: the full roster across the three lanes with the lane leader pinned, live computed status per specialist, a pulse strip (in flight now, completed today, average turnaround, this week), and a recent wins feed. What the client does with it: watches their organization work the way an owner walks the floor, and shows it to partners and staff to explain how the business runs now. Why it is hard to copy: the page reads only real Desk data and is bound by honesty rules in code (status is computed from open projects, never set by hand; the page never selects internal fields). A competitor can draw a prettier chart; they cannot fake the underlying work stream that makes the chart move.

**A3. Request intake.** What it is: the submit surface inside the app, plus the two alternate modes (the client's own project tool connected by webhook or poll, or a direct message to the lead), all normalizing into one pipeline: raw request stored, dedupe, brief rewrite, project created, lane routed, client acknowledged. What the client does with it: asks in plain words, in whichever surface they already live in, and gets an acknowledgment with the project visible on their board. Why it is hard to copy: the intake is the front of the brief standard. The rewrite from a raw ask into an execution brief (objective, system of record, current state to verify, exact changes, verification, definition of done) is where most AI delegation products quietly fail, and it is a trained team behavior backed by the Pack, not a form field.

**A4. The Output Ledger.** What it is: a running, system-generated record of every completed deliverable, with date, category, and turnaround, and a monthly impact summary for the owner. Fully specified in its own section below. What the client does with it: answers "what has this team actually done for us" at any moment, in renewal conversations, in partner meetings, and in their own planning. Why it is hard to copy: the ledger is trustworthy because it cannot be edited by the people it evaluates. Entries are generated by the completion event in the Desk, corrections happen only through a reopen, and nothing is counted that was not delivered. Output proof that cannot be inflated is a different product from a monthly report someone assembles in a slide deck.

**A5. Digests.** What it is: the one minute daily digest (what finished, what is in flight, what needs the client, if anything) and the weekly review (ledger highlights, open items, next priorities), generated from Desk state. What the client does with it: stays informed in one minute a day without opening the app, and makes the few decisions that are genuinely theirs from inside the digest. Why it is hard to copy: digests are only calm when the board underneath is disciplined. Because the Desk is the single source of truth and client summaries are a required field at completion, the digest writes itself from facts. Firms without that discipline send status theater instead.

## Group B: The team

**B1. Named specialists across three lanes.** What it is: a roster of named specialists (CRM, data, marketing, creative, voice, web, SEO and AEO, meetings, call QA, A2P compliance, finance support, performance analysis, growth, client success, memory, SOPs, delivery QA, fulfillment, onboarding), each placed in the lane where that work runs best: Muse, Claude, or Grok. What the client does with it: nothing directly, and that is the point; they see the roster on the heartbeat page and in delivery summaries, so work arrives with a name attached. Why it is hard to copy: a specialist on this roster is not a persona. Each one stands behind a skill file, a quality checklist, and proven deliverables, and the coverage matrix marks thin areas honestly. The six gap specialists on the build list (Vega for video, Pax for paid media, Bram for data and BI, Bea for bookkeeping ops, Sable for security and compliance review, Ivo for integrations) do not appear on any client roster until they have evidenced their quality bar. Roster credibility built this way takes engagements, not a weekend, to replicate.

**B2. One lead, single point of contact.** What it is: the lane leader (Maive on the Muse lane) owns the client relationship end to end: intake, routing, sequencing, escalation, and reporting. What the client does with it: talks to one person who knows their business, in their preferred channel, and never manages a specialist, a lane, or a handoff. Why it is hard to copy: the lead works because the claim protocol and routing rules behind them are codified in the Team Pack, so quality does not depend on which individual is on shift. A competitor hiring one good operator gets a person; this bundle delivers a role with a system behind it.

**B3. Team Pack updates.** What it is: the Craft layer, versioned and pushed to every install like a software update: improved checklists, new routing rules, new specialist skills, fixed failure modes learned anywhere in the fleet. What the client does with it: nothing, and their team simply gets better; update notes surface in the weekly review in client language (what their team can now do, or do better). Why it is hard to copy: the update pipeline is fed by every install UpSurge operates, sanitized through the checklist in the Team Pack document before anything moves. Each client's team improves at the rate of the whole fleet's learning, while no client's information travels with a lesson. That flywheel needs a fleet and a discipline; it cannot be bought as a tool.

## Group C: Their business memory

What it is: the Client layer memory created at install and compounded with every engagement: business profile, brand kit with approved claims, people and roles, systems map, standing rules and preferences, decisions, and the work log index. Every seeded fact carries its source; unverified facts are marked as unverified until confirmed. What the client does with it: stops repeating themselves. Month three requests start from everything the team already knows, and new staff on the client's side inherit the same record. Why it is hard to copy: the memory compounds only through work actually done inside the install, structured by the client memory template and guarded by the two layer contract (client content never leaves the install; generalized lessons leave only after sanitization and Maive's approval). It is also the client's asset: on exit, the client memory exports with them in full, in readable files. We keep clients with results, not by holding their knowledge hostage, and saying so in the bundle is itself a differentiator.

## Group D: The connections

What it is: the client's tools (CRM, email, calendar, phone and voice platform, ads, documents, payments as scoped at install) connected during the Connect stage and maintained for the life of the service: connection health watched, expirations and reauthorizations handled before they break work, and changes logged. Alongside them, the client's AI platform seats (Claude, Grok, Muse on the client owned model) receive the Team Pack bootstrap and pass the four part verification drill at install: memory recall, routing and Pack version readback, a sourced fact read, and an isolation refusal test. What the client does with it: signs in on each tool's own page during a fifteen minute checklist at install, then nothing; they never share a password with us, and they can revoke any connection at any time, instantly. Why it is hard to copy: connection maintenance is where installed AI quietly dies in most businesses, through an expired token nobody noticed. The drill and the refusal test turn "we set it up" into "we proved it works and proved it cannot leak," and the audit log keeps that proof. Maintenance discipline over months is an operations capability, not a setup wizard.

## Group E: UpSurge oversight

Summary only here; the full mechanics live in the sales-to-install ops bridge. What it is: UpSurge watches every install as a fleet: per install metrics (time to first value, turnaround, QA pass rate, escalation count), a fixed format weekly oversight report across all installs, red flags that trigger same day human attention, and a human escalation path from the client to UpSurge leadership that never routes through a ticket queue. What the client experiences: a standing weekly review, honest exception reporting (completions, blockers, material changes, nothing else), and the knowledge that a firm, not a tool vendor, is accountable for their team. Why it is hard to copy: oversight is the service. Anyone can install agents; standing behind a fleet of them with named metrics, a report format, and same day intervention is an operating commitment with real labor behind it, and it is the reason the guarantees in Group F can be offered at all.

## Group F: The guarantees and standards

**F1. Same-day completion standard.** Standard requests picked up today are finished today. When the work itself says otherwise, the client hears why and when, from the lead, the same day, not at the deadline. Due dates are not a prioritization signal inside the team; intake order and client impact are.
**F2. QA gate on every deliverable.** Nothing reaches the client on the builder's word. The gate is: builder self check against the deliverable type's checklist, independent verification against the live system or the rendered artifact, inspection of the client facing surface in its final form, and a release record on the project. Exceptions require the client's own approval noted on the project, and an exception never becomes a precedent.
**F3. Data isolation.** Client facts, data, names, and work product never enter the Craft layer, never appear in another install, and never render on any surface but their own. Internal fields (notes, briefs, blockers) are structurally separated from client fields in the Desk schema, and the heartbeat page and ledger read client fields only, enforced by a leak test in QA.
**F4. No internal process exposure.** The client sees capability, status, and results. They never see prompts, delegation mechanics, tooling, or internal discussion, and client facing language is checked for it. Their experience is a firm delivering work, not a machine being operated.

Why this group is hard to copy: each standard is falsifiable by the client's own ledger and board. Guarantees a client can audit from their own app are a commitment device competitors avoid, because their data would not survive the same audit.

## Group G: Enablement

**G1. Staff quick-start.** A one page card for the client's team: who the lead is, the three ways to submit a request, what happens next, and where to watch it. Fifteen minutes to brief a whole staff, no training session required.
**G2. Good-request guide.** The short client guide from the onboarding pack: plain words are fine, name the outcome and the deadline if there is one, attach the source material if it exists, two worked examples included. Better inputs visibly produce faster outputs, and clients can see that in their own turnaround numbers.
**G3. Lead access.** Office-hours style access to the lead inside the weekly review plus an always open message channel to the lead for requests and questions, with UpSurge leadership behind the lead for anything escalated. Access is to a person accountable for their account, not to a support queue.

Why it is hard to copy: enablement is what turns an installed tool into an adopted one. Most AI deployments fail at the client's staff, not at the model. Shipping the adoption kit as part of the bundle is an admission that the product includes the humans on the client's side, and it raises the client's usage, which raises their ledger, which is the renewal argument.

## The Output Ledger: feature spec

The ledger is a surface inside the client's Command App, generated entirely from Desk data (the `requests` table and the `team_roster` table, using the fields added by the heartbeat schema). It has two views: the running ledger and the monthly impact summary.

**Ledger entry fields.** One entry per completed project, written by the completion event, never by hand:

- `completed_at`: the timestamp the project status became Done, set by the app in the same update, cleared if the project is ever reopened.
- Deliverable title: the project title, with the `client_summary` (1 to 3 sentences, what was delivered and where to find it) shown beneath it. No summary, no entry: the same publish rule the heartbeat wins feed already enforces.
- Category: a controlled list on the project, set at triage, one value per project: Voice agent, CRM workflow, Landing page, Video, Report, Ad campaign, Content, Data, Integration, Operations. This requires one additive column, `category`, alongside the heartbeat schema additions.
- Turnaround: computed, never entered: `completed_at` minus `requested_at`, displayed in the house format (under 60 minutes in minutes, under 24 hours in hours to one decimal, otherwise days to one decimal).
- Lane and specialist: from `lane` and the `team_roster` join on `specialist_id`, shown as the name the client sees on their team page.
- Deliverable link: the project's result links (the page, file, recording, or record the client received), so every entry is one tap from its evidence.
- Client visibility: entries exist only for projects with `client_visible = true`.

**Monthly impact summary.** Generated on the first of the month for the month just closed, from the same rows, no separate data entry: total deliverables completed, count by category, median turnaround, percentage of standard requests completed the same day they were requested, and the three largest deliverables by client summary prominence (most recent first, ties by completion order). The summary carries no estimated dollar value, no hours-saved figure, and no claim the data cannot prove. If the owner wants a value conversation, the lead has it in the weekly review using the client's own numbers, not a formula in the app.

**Rules, binding.**

1. Nothing manual. An entry exists because a project completed with a client summary, and for no other reason. Nobody can add, edit, or backdate an entry.
2. Reopen means removal. If a project reopens, its entry leaves the ledger and its completion timestamp clears; if it completes again, the new entry carries the new date and the full elapsed time from the original request. The ledger never shows a turnaround the client did not actually experience.
3. Internal work never appears. Test projects and internal only work are `client_visible = false` and excluded from the ledger, the monthly summary, and every heartbeat count, by the same filter.
4. Nothing inflated, by construction. No duplicate counting (one project, one entry), no partial completions (a project is Done or it is not in the ledger), no estimates, no imported history from before the install except the install project itself, which is a real Desk project and the ledger's legitimate first entry.
5. The ledger is exportable by the client at any time (the full record, readable file), consistent with the memory export promise in Group C.

**Why it matters commercially.** The ledger converts the service from a monthly act of faith into an audited record the owner can open mid-conversation. It is the renewal document, the expansion document, and the answer to the one question every owner eventually asks.

## The showcase: how the app sells the service

**S1. The demonstration organization.** A standing demo workspace in the Command App, populated with a fictional business (name, roster, board, and ledger invented for the demo and labeled as a demonstration inside the workspace) whose board shows realistic completed work across every category: a voice agent live, a CRM workflow delivered, a landing page shipped, a month of ledger history with honest turnaround spread, including one project that took two days with the reason visible in its summary. Prospects see the whole rhythm: intake, brief, movement on the team page, delivery, ledger entry. The demo workspace runs on real app code and real Desk queries; only the business is fictional. Nothing in it is a screenshot or a mockup, because the first prospect who taps something unexpected should find that it works.

**S2. Our own board is the pattern, never the exhibit.** UpSurge runs its own operations on this same system, and the sales conversation says so plainly: the team selling this is the team that runs on it. What prospects may see of our install is the shape only: the heartbeat page layout, the ledger format, category counts with project titles and client identities never shown. No client project, title, summary, or figure from any real install is ever used in a demo, a deck, or a call. Client data isolation (F3) applies to sales material with the same force as to the product, and the demo workspace exists precisely so nobody is ever tempted to borrow a real board.

**S3. The live demo sequence.** The ten minute demo script in the sales asset pack runs on the demonstration organization: open on the heartbeat page while work is visibly in flight, submit a real request in front of the prospect and show the brief appear on the board, open one finished deliverable from its ledger entry, then close on the monthly impact summary. The prospect's own first question ("what would you hand off first") is answered by pointing at the category list, not by a feature tour.

**S4. Every client's ledger becomes their renewal evidence.** By design, no renewal conversation starts from a pitch. It starts from the client's own monthly summaries: deliverables by category, median turnaround, same-day percentage, quarter over quarter. Expansion follows the same evidence: the category the ledger shows growing (content volume, campaign count, locations added) is the tier conversation, and a client approaching a tier's working limits sees it in their own numbers before we raise it. Churn defense is also ledger shaped: a quiet month is visible to UpSurge oversight in the weekly fleet report weeks before it becomes a cancellation, which is when the lead reopens the "what would you hand off" conversation with specifics from their own history.

## What is NOT included

Stated cleanly, in the bundle and in the agreement, so the offer stays honest:

- **Third party subscription and usage costs.** The client's platform seats (Claude, Grok, Muse), their CRM, phone and voice usage, ad spend, and any connected tool's fees are the client's, paid to those providers, unless the client is on a managed arrangement explicitly priced to include them. We never mark these up invisibly.
- **Human staff work.** The team is an AI organization with UpSurge oversight. Physical tasks, on site work, licensed professional services (legal, accounting, medical, brokerage acts reserved to licensees), and anything requiring a human body or a human license are out of scope; where the team prepares work for a licensed human (drafts, briefs, assembled files), the human of record remains the client's.
- **Work outside the connected systems.** The team works in the systems connected at install and added by agreement. A platform the client has not connected, or will not grant access to, is a platform the team cannot act in; the connect checklist is the boundary, and it is visible on day one.
- **Undisclosed data and decisions.** The team cannot use information the client has not provided or connected, and decisions reserved to the owner (pricing to their customers, hiring, contracts, anything flagged as a client decision in their standing rules) are prepared for, never taken. The board shows these as waiting on the client, in plain words.
- **Guarantees about third parties.** Platform uptime, carrier and A2P approvals, ad platform reviews, and provider policy decisions are outside our control. The standards in Group F govern our work; where a third party blocks it, the client sees the blocker and our workaround the same day, per the escalation rules.

## The bundle by tier

Tier names and positioning are from the sales asset pack. Numbers are Austin's to set; this section allocates the bundle only.

**In every tier (the floor, never negotiated down):** the Command App suite (board A1, heartbeat page A2, intake A3 in the app mode, Output Ledger A4, daily digest from A5), one lead as single point of contact (B2), business memory (Group C) with exit export, connections made and maintained for the tools in scope at install (Group D), UpSurge oversight at the standard rhythm (Group E), all four guarantees and standards (Group F), and the full enablement kit (Group G). The floor is high on purpose: the proof machinery (ledger, heartbeat, QA gate, isolation) is what makes the service believable, and it is never the upgrade.

**Launch Team** (the operational floor covered): the floor above, with the core specialist bench for everyday operations (inbox, scheduling, follow up, reporting, data care) across the lanes those tasks route to, intake by app and direct message, the weekly review in digest form, and request volume sized for one location and one brand.

**Growth Team** (everything in Launch Team, for the business actively acquiring customers): the marketing and sales support bench unlocks in full (content and SEO/AEO specialists, creative production, campaign work, voice agents built and run at volume, pipeline and follow up systems), the client's own project tool as a third intake mode, the live weekly review call with the lead, and volume sized for active acquisition across one brand.

**Full AI Organization** (everything in Growth Team, for multi-location and multi-brand operators): expanded capacity and priority turnaround (Full AI Organization projects sequence ahead of standard queue order across the fleet's shared specialists, stated plainly so the promise is understood), multiple Desk workspaces or brand separated boards with one consolidated ledger view for the owner, a dedicated oversight rhythm with the UpSurge senior team (monthly strategy session and quarterly review built from the client's ledger), additional locations and brands onboarded under the same memory contract, and first access to new Pack capabilities and newly proven specialists as they join the roster.

Two allocation rules protect the edge at every tier. First, capability upgrades through Team Pack updates (B3) reach all tiers; tiers buy capacity, bench breadth, and oversight depth, never the withholding of a fix or a learned improvement. Second, nothing in a higher tier is a promise a lower tier's data contradicts: priority turnaround at Full AI Organization is real precisely because standard turnaround at Launch Team is already measured and published in every client's own ledger.
