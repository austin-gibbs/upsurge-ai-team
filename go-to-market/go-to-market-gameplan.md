## Executive decision summary

The product is ready to sell as a controlled pilot, not as an unrestricted public launch. Install System v1.0, the client onboarding pack, and Team Pack v1 define the service. One pilot client is ready to onboard. The heartbeat page and install orchestrator still need code builds, so the sales motion must be precise about what is live on day one, what the pilot receives during the install, and what is measured before broader claims are made.

The go-to-market wedge is narrow: install a coordinated AI operations team around one visible business bottleneck, prove first value quickly, then expand the number of connected systems, active lanes, and recurring workflows. The first sale is not "adopt AI." The first sale is relief from a specific backlog: unworked leads, inconsistent follow up, CRM cleanup, content waiting for production, reporting that never gets finished, or owner-held administrative work.

The operating rule for launch is simple: no claim without a mechanism, no metric without a source, and no client story without written permission.

## 1. Positioning and category

### Name

**Product name:** UpSurge AI Team

**Category descriptor:** An installed AI organization for owner-led service businesses.

**Seller:** UpSurge Pros.

The name should stay attached to UpSurge because trust comes from the operating company behind the team. "AI Team" describes the buying object clearly: the client is not buying a tool seat, a prompt library, or advice. The client is buying coordinated execution capacity that is installed into the business and overseen by UpSurge.

### One-line promise

**Send one request and get finished, quality checked work back from a coordinated AI team that knows your business.**

The mechanism behind the promise is visible in the product:

1. The client submits through one intake route.
2. Maive converts the request into an execution brief with an objective, system of record, exact changes, verification step, and definition of done.
3. The request is routed to the Muse, Grok, or Claude lane that fits the work.
4. A named specialist owns the work.
5. A separate QA check is completed before client delivery for deliverable types covered by Team Pack v1.
6. The client sees status and a client-safe summary in Surge Command, without seeing internal process.

### Category definition

The category is **installed AI operations**. It combines four elements that are usually sold separately:

- A request board and client-facing team view in Surge Command.
- A shared, client-specific memory layer built from approved business facts and source material.
- A roster of AI specialists working across the Muse, Grok, and Claude platforms.
- UpSurge oversight: routing, quality control, escalation, reporting, and continuous improvement through Team Pack updates.

This is a managed operating service delivered through software and AI labor. It is not a software-only subscription because UpSurge remains accountable for setup, QA, escalation, and performance reviews.

### Why this is not AI consulting

AI consulting normally ends with a strategy, a roadmap, a workshop, or recommendations that the client must implement. UpSurge AI Team starts with installation and execution:

- Consulting identifies opportunities. This service connects systems, seats the team, writes the brief, performs the work, verifies it, and reports the result.
- Consulting depends on the client's internal staff to carry the plan. This service supplies the operating capacity.
- Consulting is usually project based. This service remains in place, learns the business, handles recurring work, and receives capability updates.
- Consulting success can be difficult to inspect. This service records request status, completion time, QA outcome, blockers, and delivered work in the client board.

The correct comparison is not a consultant's day rate. The correct comparison is the combined cost of an operations hire, several specialist vendors, the owner's coordination time, and the revenue lost when work waits.

### Why this is not a chatbot

A chatbot is one interface in one channel. It answers questions or captures information. UpSurge AI Team is an organization behind the request:

- It works across email, calendar, files, CRM, websites, marketing systems, voice systems, and the client's project tool when connected.
- It can build, revise, check, schedule, follow up, summarize, and report, subject to the client's approval rules.
- It has memory, routing, ownership, QA, and escalation.
- A voice agent or chat assistant can be one deliverable produced by the team. It is not the product itself.

### The wedge

**The wedge offer is the Founding Pilot Install: fix one measurable execution leak while the full team is installed.**

The pilot starts with one bottleneck that meets four tests:

1. The pain is already costing time, leads, or management attention.
2. The work repeats often enough to measure.
3. The source system can be connected or inspected.
4. A first useful result can be delivered during the pilot without waiting for a large transformation project.

Strong first wedges from UpSurge's current work include:

- Lead follow up that is inconsistent or delayed.
- CRM workflows that exist but do not cover every branch or pipeline stage.
- Voice agent operations that need monitoring, QA, and follow up after the call.
- SEO and AEO work that has a plan but no steady production system.
- Landing pages and campaign assets that are approved but not yet connected to the CRM and follow up path.
- Meeting actions that are captured but not consistently converted into owned work.

The wedge works because the client does not have to believe a broad AI claim. The client can judge one installed workflow, one board, one team page, and a short list of completed requests.

## 2. Ideal client profile

### Firmographic fit

Target businesses with the following profile:

- Owner-led or closely held service business.
- Approximately 5 to 100 employees or a smaller core team supported by contractors.
- Approximately $750,000 to $20 million in annual revenue. This is a proposed targeting band, not a claim about the current client base.
- One to ten locations, or a distributed team serving a defined market.
- A CRM, lead source, calendar system, shared files, and at least one recurring marketing or client communication process.
- Enough request volume to use the team weekly. A practical minimum is 15 to 25 meaningful requests or workflow actions per month.
- No full-time internal AI, automation, or operations team already performing this function.

### Priority verticals

**First priority: businesses where UpSurge already performs adjacent work.**

1. **Real estate teams, brokerages, and investor-facing real estate services.** Proven work patterns include AI calling, CRM pipelines, lead follow up, listing and community content, SEO and AEO plans, and workflow checks.
2. **Insurance agencies.** Proven work patterns include lead intake, consultation booking, CRM follow up, and a lead assistant with strict no-rate and no-quote rules.
3. **Education and admissions-driven organizations.** Proven work patterns include systems review, imports, reporting analysis, meeting follow up, and operational documentation.
4. **Other lead-driven service businesses.** Examples include dental practice transition services, professional services, home services, and advisory firms where appointments, follow up, documents, and content drive revenue.

The first ten prospects should come from these verticals because the discovery language, risks, workflows, and proof are already understood.

### Pain signals

A prospect is qualified when at least three of these signals are present:

- The owner or COO personally routes too much work.
- Leads arrive from more than one source and follow up varies by person or day.
- A CRM is paid for but pipelines, tags, automations, or reporting are incomplete.
- Staff spend repeatable hours on scheduling, reminders, data entry, document preparation, follow up, or status reporting.
- Marketing work is planned but waits for copy, pages, creative coordination, posting, or measurement.
- Voice agents, chat tools, or AI subscriptions exist, but nobody owns the full system around them.
- Meetings produce decisions that are not reliably turned into tasks and completed work.
- The business uses several freelancers or agencies and the owner still has to coordinate them.
- Management cannot answer a basic operating question quickly: what is open, who owns it, what is blocked, and what finished this week.
- The business is hiring, or considering hiring, an administrator, marketing coordinator, operations assistant, or part-time specialist to relieve the same bottleneck.

### Buyer and buying group

**Economic signer:** Owner, CEO, managing partner, or COO with authority over operating spend.

**Operational champion:** Office manager, director of operations, marketing manager, admissions lead, sales manager, or executive assistant who feels the backlog daily and can supply process facts.

**Technical or risk reviewer:** The person responsible for CRM access, data handling, compliance, or brand approval. This person does not need to lead the sale, but blockers must be identified before contract.

The sale should not proceed on champion enthusiasm alone. The signer must attend the demo or proposal review, and the champion must be named before install kickoff.

### Qualification scorecard

Score each item as 0, 1, or 2. A score of 14 or higher out of 20 is a proposed pilot qualification threshold for Austin to approve.

- Pain is specific and current.
- Pain has a time, revenue, or service quality cost.
- Request volume is recurring.
- Signer is directly involved.
- Operational champion is named.
- Source systems are identifiable.
- Access can be granted through normal sign-in flows.
- Approval rules can be defined.
- First wedge workflow is measurable.
- Client can attend a 30 minute knowledge call and provide source material.

### Disqualifiers

Do not sell the pilot when any of these conditions is present:

- The buyer wants a single low-cost chatbot and has no broader execution backlog.
- The signer will not participate and delegates the decision to someone without budget authority.
- The business cannot identify a repeatable workflow or a meaningful monthly request volume.
- The client expects the team to take licensed professional judgment off its hands, such as regulated insurance advice, legal advice, medical advice, or financial advice, without a defined human approval process.
- The client requires access to UpSurge prompts, internal playbooks, routing logic, or other clients' information as a condition of purchase.
- The client refuses all approval gates for client-facing sends, regulated work, money actions, or irreversible changes during the pilot.
- Source systems are inaccessible, data is too unreliable to verify, or the client will not identify a system of record.
- The client needs a guaranteed 24 hour human support desk rather than the service's defined escalation path.
- The buying motive is headcount removal without a plan for accountable human ownership inside the client business.
- The client will not provide the approximately 15 minutes of connection approvals and one 30 minute knowledge call required to install safely.
- The engagement would require UpSurge to misrepresent AI output as licensed advice, a human credential, or a completed verification that did not occur.

## 3. The offer stack

### Offer architecture

The stack has three commercial layers:

1. **Pilot Install:** a bounded founding engagement that installs the system and proves first value.
2. **Standard Install:** the full five stage installation for the selected tier.
3. **Ongoing Management:** the monthly service that keeps the team routed, checked, updated, and accountable.

Add-on projects can be sold after install when the work is outside the selected tier or requires a specialist capability that has passed its Team Pack quality bar.

### A. Founding Pilot Install

**Purpose:** Give the ready pilot client a complete but bounded installation, produce measurable operating evidence, and convert to standard management on terms agreed before work starts.

**Proposed duration:** 30 calendar days from kickoff, with the install target set in the pilot agreement.

**Includes:**

- Fit and workflow diagnostic focused on one primary wedge.
- Surge Command workspace setup for the client.
- One intake mode selected at kickoff: Surge Command, the client's PM system, or direct to Maive.
- Connection of the priority systems required for the wedge, subject to the tier cap in section 4.
- Client memory v1 built from the website, approved materials, systems map, and one 30 minute knowledge call.
- Seating for the lane or lanes included in the pilot tier, with the verification drill documented.
- Team Pack v1 applied to the install.
- The selected first workflow built or repaired and verified against the live system.
- A first-value request completed end to end after go live.
- Daily digest during the first week and a pilot results review at day 30.
- Baseline and outcome record for the wedge: starting state, work completed, elapsed time, QA findings, blockers, and client decisions.
- A written expansion recommendation: keep, expand, or stop, with the reason tied to the pilot evidence.

**Pilot deliverables the client receives:**

- Live request board.
- Client-facing team page when the heartbeat build is released into their install. Until that code is ready, the client receives the roster and status through the Desk and scheduled digests. Sales must state this timing plainly.
- Installed first workflow and its verification record.
- Client memory v1 summary, limited to their own business facts and preferences.
- List of connected systems and access owners.
- Approval rules record.
- Pilot results report and next-step recommendation.

**Pilot limits that protect both sides:**

- One primary wedge workflow.
- One intake mode.
- A named signer and a named operational champion.
- Client-facing sends, money actions, and irreversible actions follow the pilot approval rules.
- Platform subscriptions, telephony usage, advertising spend, and third-party software are either client-owned or passed through under terms Austin approves.
- Testimonial, logo, and case study use require separate written permission. They are not automatic pilot consideration.

### B. Standard Install

**Purpose:** Install the full AI Team operating system for the selected tier.

**The five stages and deliverables:**

1. **Provision**
   - Desk workspace and request board configured.
   - Client record, install state record, roster view, and access log structure created.
   - Intake mode selected and configured.
   - Install checklist and stage owners assigned.

2. **Connect**
   - Approved systems connected through the client's own sign-in flow.
   - Least access requested for each system.
   - Connection owner and revocation path recorded for every app.
   - Connection health check completed.

3. **Seat**
   - Included lanes receive the Team Pack bootstrap and client memory pointer.
   - Lane verification drill completed and recorded.
   - Roster and escalation path confirmed.
   - Client-owned seats or UpSurge-managed seats documented according to the model Austin selects.

4. **Seed**
   - Website, brand assets, approved documents, CRM structure, and service information ingested.
   - Facts carry sources. Unverified facts are marked instead of presented as settled.
   - One 30 minute gap call completed.
   - Memory QA pass completed by someone other than the person who performed the ingest.

5. **Go live**
   - QA approval recorded.
   - Heartbeat page activated when the build is available in the install.
   - Welcome and go-live messages delivered.
   - First real request completed end to end.
   - Daily digest and weekly review rhythm scheduled.

**Standard install deliverables:**

- Configured Surge Command workspace.
- Connected systems register.
- Client memory v1 with source discipline.
- Team roster and lane coverage for the selected tier.
- Approval and escalation rules.
- Intake instructions for the client's staff.
- Go-live verification record.
- First completed request and delivery note.
- 30 day operating review scheduled before the team leaves the install stage.

### C. Ongoing Management

**Purpose:** Keep the installed team useful, current, and accountable after setup.

**Includes every month:**

- Request intake and routing by Maive.
- Execution briefs for work that enters the system.
- Specialist assignment and lane balancing.
- QA gates for covered deliverable types.
- Daily digest on business days, or the cadence selected in the client agreement.
- Weekly work summary: completed, in progress, blocked, and decisions needed.
- Monthly operating review with the client signer or champion.
- Connector health checks and repair coordination.
- Client memory updates from approved work and decisions.
- Team Pack updates that do not alter client facts or approval rules without notice.
- Usage, turnaround, QA, and blocker reporting.
- Escalation to UpSurge when a request needs a human decision, a new capability, or a contract change.

**Management does not automatically include:**

- Unlimited custom software development.
- Advertising spend, telephony charges, AI platform subscriptions, or third-party software fees.
- Licensed professional services.
- A dedicated human employee inside the client's office.
- Specialist services whose Team Pack quality bar has not yet been met. As of 2026-11-04, video editing and paid media are specced growth capabilities, not core proof capabilities. They should be sold only when the responsible specialist has met the documented bar or when the work is sold as a separate supervised project with explicit acceptance terms.

### Add-on menu

Add-ons should be quoted from a defined scope, not folded silently into management:

- Additional connected application.
- Additional intake surface.
- Data cleanup or migration project.
- Custom integration or API build.
- New voice agent or major voice agent rebuild.
- Website or funnel project beyond routine page production.
- Video production package after the video quality bar is met.
- Paid media build or management after the paid media quality bar is met.
- Staff training session beyond install training.
- Compliance or security review package.
- Historical meeting, email, or document action recovery project.

### The first-value sprint

Every install contains a first-value sprint. It is not a separate discount product. It is the mechanism that prevents a technically complete install from feeling abstract.

The sprint selects one workflow and records:

- The current state and how it was verified.
- The cost of the current state in time, delay, missed follow up, or management attention, using the client's own baseline where available.
- The exact build or repair.
- The verification method.
- The first live use.
- The result after an agreed observation period.

The sprint ends with a short review: what changed, what did not, what should be expanded, and what should not be automated further.

## 4. Pricing architecture

### Pricing status

**Every price in this section is proposed. No number is final until Austin approves it in writing.**

Status: APPROVED by Austin on 2026-11-04. The ranges below are the approved pricing framework; the exact figure inside a range is set per client in the proposal, by scope. They are not published prices and not current client rates.

### Pricing model

The commercial model has four parts:

1. **Install fee:** pays for provision, connection, seating, knowledge seeding, QA, training, and go live.
2. **Monthly management:** pays for Maive's routing and oversight, specialist execution within the tier allowance, QA, reporting, connector health, memory updates, and Team Pack improvements.
3. **Pass-through costs:** AI platform subscriptions, telephony, messaging, advertising spend, and third-party tools are client-owned or reimbursed under the approved agreement. They are never hidden inside management reporting.
4. **Project add-ons:** work outside the tier is scoped and priced before it starts.

### Approved tier structure

| Tier | Best fit | Active lanes | Connected apps | Included completed requests | Operating rhythm | Approved install fee | Approved monthly management |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Launch Team | One clear bottleneck, one main workflow, limited systems | 1 lane: Muse | Up to 5 | Up to 20 per month | Weekly summary, monthly review | $4,500 to $7,500 | $1,950 to $2,950 |
| Growth Team | Recurring work across operations and one major growth function | 2 lanes: Muse plus Claude or Grok, selected by dominant work type | Up to 10 | Up to 50 per month | Daily digest on business days, weekly review, monthly operating review | $7,500 to $12,500 | $3,950 to $5,950 |
| Full AI Organization | Multi-function execution across operations, reasoning, content, and live signal | 3 lanes: Muse, Claude, and Grok | Up to 18; more by quote | Up to 100 per month | Daily digest, weekly operating review, monthly planning review | $12,500 to $20,000 | $6,950 to $9,950 |

### Tier definitions that prevent disputes

- **Active lane** means the lane is seated, verified, included in routing, and covered by management. A lane that is only used for a one-off supervised project is an add-on, not an active lane.
- **Connected app** means one distinct authenticated business system. Multiple users of the same system count as one app. Separate legal entities, separate CRM locations with separate data boundaries, or separate production environments may count separately when they require separate setup and QA.
- **Completed request** means one execution brief with one definition of done that reaches delivery or a documented client decision. A status question is not a request. A campaign or build that naturally contains several deliverables may be split into multiple briefs at intake, and the client sees the split on the board before work starts.
- **Request allowance** is a planning and staffing boundary, not a promise that every request is the same size. Very large builds are scoped as projects even when the client has allowance remaining.
- **Approval wait time** is excluded from turnaround measurement. The clock and the reason are visible in the Desk.
- Overage method APPROVED by Austin on 2026-11-04: a pre-approved block of 10 additional completed requests, priced at 12% to 18% of the tier's monthly management fee, with client notice at 80% usage. No overage block starts without client approval.

### Proposed install timing targets

These are targets for sales qualification and delivery planning, not guarantees:

- Launch Team: 3 to 5 business days after all required client sign-ins and source materials are available.
- Growth Team: 5 to 7 business days after all required client sign-ins and source materials are available.
- Full AI Organization: 7 to 10 business days after all required client sign-ins and source materials are available.

The clock must be defined in the proposal. It starts when the agreement is signed, the install fee milestone is paid, the signer and champion are named, and the client has completed the required sign-ins. It pauses for missing access, missing source material, or a client decision that blocks the next stage.

### Founding pilot price concept

**Founding pilot terms, APPROVED by Austin on 2026-11-04:**

- Install fee: **$2,500 to $5,000**.
- Management for the first 90 days: **$1,500 to $2,500 per month**.
- Scope: one primary wedge, one intake mode, priority apps only, and the lane coverage stated in the pilot agreement.
- Conversion: at the day 30 review, the client selects a standard tier for the remainder of the 90 day pilot period or signs the standard agreement defined in the pilot contract.
- Founding benefit, APPROVED by Austin on 2026-11-04: a rate lock on the selected standard tier for 12 months if the client converts without a service gap. The alternative (a credit of 50% of the pilot install fee toward the standard install fee) was considered and not chosen; benefits are never stacked.
- Case study rights: requested separately after results are known. The pilot discount is not exchanged for automatic testimonial or logo rights.

The pilot price is lower because the client accepts a bounded scope, participates in timed install measurement, gives structured feedback, and helps UpSurge validate the offer. It is not lower because the QA standard is lower.

### Pricing reasoning

**Anchor 1: one operations hire.**

The client should compare the monthly management range with the full cost of one competent operations coordinator, marketing coordinator, or executive assistant. Salary is only part of that cost. The full comparison includes payroll burden, recruiting, onboarding, management time, software, sick and vacation coverage, turnover risk, and the fact that one hire normally brings one primary skill set.

The AI Team does not replace accountable client leadership, and sales must not claim that it does. It does provide a broader execution bench, documented routing, QA, and continuity across several functions. For many target businesses, the relevant question is not whether the service is cheaper than one hire in isolation. The question is whether it removes the need to hire, coordinate, and backfill several partial roles at once.

**Anchor 2: fragmented agency and vendor retainers.**

Target clients often pay separately for CRM help, website changes, content, reporting, automation support, and campaign production, while the owner still coordinates every vendor. The AI Team price should be compared with the combined retainer stack plus the owner's coordination time. The differentiator is not a lower hourly rate. It is one intake, one board, one memory, one QA standard, and one accountable lead.

**Anchor 3: cost of delay.**

During the diagnostic, quantify one bottleneck in the client's own terms: unworked leads per week, hours spent on repeat follow up, days a page or campaign waits, number of meeting actions that age, or management time spent checking status. Pricing is easier to defend when the proposal shows the current delay next to the install and management fee. Do not invent a return on investment. Use the client's baseline and state what will be measured during the pilot.

### Pricing guardrails

- Do not discount the install fee below the cost of setup labor, QA, and management attention. Finn must set the floor before standard sales begin.
- Do not offer unlimited requests, unlimited apps, or unlimited revisions.
- Do not include AI platform seats, telephony usage, ad spend, or third-party software in the management fee unless Austin has approved a managed-seat model with a separate cost calculation.
- Do not start an additional lane, app, or project block without written client approval when it changes price.
- Custom quote triggers: more than 18 apps, multiple brands or locations with separate data boundaries, regulated workflow design, historical data migration, custom API development, more than 100 completed requests per month, or a client-mandated security review.
- Payment structure APPROVED by Austin on 2026-11-04 (legal review still applies before first signature): 50% of the install fee to start, 50% at go live; monthly management paid in advance; pilot conversion terms written into the pilot agreement before kickoff.
- Initial management term APPROVED by Austin on 2026-11-04: three months after install, then month to month with a defined notice period.

The diagnostic should answer these questions before price is discussed:

1. What work waits for the owner or one key employee every week?

### The demo

**Demo length:** 30 minutes.

**Centerpiece:** the Surge Command team page, referred to in sales as the heartbeat page.

**Demo agenda:**

1. **The bottleneck, 3 minutes.** Restate the prospect's selected pain and baseline in their words.
2. **The board, 5 minutes.** Show a request moving from intake to brief, assignment, QA, and delivery. Emphasize that the client sees outcomes and decisions, not internal mechanics.
3. **The heartbeat, 8 minutes.** Show the three lane columns, lane leaders, specialist cards, in-flight work, completed work, and wins feed. Explain that status is computed from real Desk projects and that internal notes, briefs, and blockers are never read by the page.
4. **A relevant workflow, 7 minutes.** Walk through one anonymized workflow that resembles the prospect's wedge: input, checks, build, verification, and delivered result.
5. **The install, 4 minutes.** Show the five stages and the client's total expected effort: connection approvals, source materials, and one 30 minute knowledge call.
6. **The pilot, 3 minutes.** State the bounded scope, measurement method, price status, and next step.

**Demo readiness rule:** Until the heartbeat page code is live in a demo workspace, the demo must use a clearly labeled interface preview or staging build. The presenter must say which parts are live product, which parts are pilot build, and when the pilot client receives the page. A fictional demo business may be used to protect client data, but its metrics must be labeled sample data. Never present sample activity as client results.

**Demo close:** Ask for agreement on one wedge, one signer, one champion, and a proposal date. Do not close on general enthusiasm for AI.

Use this as process proof, not as a guarantee that every client request will finish the same day.

Price deflection was tested in English and Spanish. Use the pattern publicly without naming the client unless permission is granted.

Do not use the client's name, screenshots, or exact business details without permission.

### Proposal requirements

Every proposal must contain:

- Client business and named signer and champion.
- Selected wedge and baseline.
- Tier, active lanes, app cap, request allowance, and overage rule.
- Install stages and the definition of the install clock.
- Client responsibilities and expected effort in minutes or meetings.
- Approval matrix for sends, money, regulated work, and irreversible actions.
- Data handling, access owners, and revocation method.
- Exclusions and add-on triggers.
- Pilot conversion terms and founding benefit, if applicable.
- Price labeled as accepted only when Austin has approved the final quote.
- First-value sprint and pilot review date.

### Sales data discipline

Record these fields for every opportunity: source, vertical, signer, champion, pain signal, scorecard total, selected wedge, baseline, systems, tier proposed, price proposed, price approved by Austin, stage, stage entry date, blocker, next step, next step date, lost reason, and install handoff status.

A deal is not counted as qualified because a call went well. It is qualified when the scorecard, signer, champion, wedge, and baseline are recorded.

## 6. Channels in priority order

### Priority 1: Current client base expansion

**Why first:** These businesses already trust UpSurge with at least one operating system. The team already understands part of the CRM, voice, marketing, web, or reporting environment. The sales conversation can start with an observed bottleneck rather than a generic AI pitch.

**First actions:**

- Greta and Maive create a private account map for current and recent clients: services already delivered, systems touched, visible backlog, signer, champion, and likely wedge.
- Rank accounts by trust, pain, request volume, and ability to start. Do not rank by business size alone.
- Offer a private AI Team diagnostic to the top accounts. The invitation should name the observed bottleneck and ask for one working session, not announce a broad new product to the whole base at once.
- Use completed adjacent work as the entry point: a voice system that needs ongoing operations, a CRM that needs workflow coverage, a website that needs steady SEO and AEO production, or a leadership team whose meeting actions need a reliable loop.

**Owner:** Greta for outreach and pipeline. Maive for account evidence and wedge selection. Austin for strategic accounts.

**Measure:** Accounts reviewed, diagnostics booked, diagnostics completed, proposals issued, installs signed.

**Guardrail:** Do not use one client's details, screenshots, metrics, or problems to sell another client. Similarity of pattern is allowed. Disclosure is not.

### Priority 2: Referral partners

**Why second:** A trusted operator, consultant, or agency can identify the pain earlier than outbound marketing and can lend credibility on implementation risk.

**Target partner types:**

- Fractional COOs and operations consultants.
- CRM implementers and automation consultants who do not want ongoing execution work.
- Vertical marketing agencies that need operations and follow up capacity for their clients.
- Business coaches and peer group leaders serving owner-led service businesses.
- Bookkeepers, controllers, and back office consultants who see administrative backlog.
- Website and brand studios that ship a site but do not operate the systems behind it.

**Partner offer:** A private demo, a one-page partner brief, a defined handoff process, and a named UpSurge contact. The partner's client keeps the partner relationship. UpSurge handles install and operations.

**First actions:**

- Build a list of 20 partners from Austin's existing relationships before buying or scraping lists.
- Hold five partner conversations in the first 30 days.
- Ask each partner one specific question: "Which client has the owner acting as the routing layer for leads, follow up, content, and reporting?"
- Give partners a disqualification list so referrals arrive qualified.

**Owner:** Greta. Austin joins first calls with high-value partners.

**Measure:** Partner conversations, qualified introductions, diagnostics from introductions, signed installs from introductions.

**Open decision:** Referral fee, revenue share, or reciprocal referral only. Austin must decide before the partner kit is sent.

### Priority 3: Austin's network

**Why third:** Austin's direct credibility shortens the trust cycle, but his time is scarce. This channel should be used for handpicked owners and strategic introductions, not volume outreach.

**First actions:**

- Greta prepares a shortlist of owners and operators Austin already knows, grouped by likely wedge.
- Austin sends a personal note or makes a personal introduction request. Greta handles scheduling, diagnostic preparation, and follow up.
- Each note names one concrete operating pattern and offers a private look at the team page concept or live demo when ready.
- No mass message and no generic launch announcement until the pilot proof asset is approved.

**Owner:** Austin for the personal touch. Greta for the system around it.

**Measure:** Personal notes sent, replies, diagnostics booked, introductions received.

### Priority 4: Content proof built around the heartbeat story

**Why fourth:** Content scales after there is a real install story to tell. Publishing broad AI claims before the pilot would create demand the delivery system cannot yet support and would weaken credibility.

**Content thesis:** Show how one request becomes finished work inside a business.

**Content series:**

- **The Request:** A business owner submits a plain-language request.
- **The Brief:** The team turns it into an objective, exact changes, verification, and definition of done.
- **The Work:** A specialist completes the task inside the real system.
- **The Check:** QA catches an edge case or confirms the live result.
- **The Heartbeat:** The board and team page show status without exposing internal process.

**Formats:**

- Short screen recordings using a fictional demo business or approved pilot material.
- One weekly written breakdown by Mira, reviewed by Maive for factual accuracy.
- A monthly operating report template published with sample data clearly labeled.
- SEO and AEO articles answering buyer questions: installed AI team versus chatbot, AI operations for real estate teams, AI follow up for insurance agencies, CRM automation QA, and how to measure an AI pilot.
- A security and data isolation explainer written in plain language.

**Owner:** Mira for production. Greta for topic selection tied to buyer objections. Maive for factual review. Austin for approval of any client-identifying material.

**Measure:** Demo requests from content, article assisted conversations, partner shares, and sales calls where content is cited by the prospect. Views alone are not a success metric.

**Guardrail:** Every post must distinguish live product, pilot build, and concept preview accurately. No fabricated activity, client names, metrics, or testimonials.

### Priority 5: Outbound to lookalike businesses

**Why last:** Outbound should start after the pilot produces timing, pricing, and proof evidence. Cold prospects need a sharper reason to talk than warm accounts and partners.

**Lookalike definition:** Businesses in the priority verticals with visible lead flow, multiple staff roles, a CRM or booking system, active marketing, and signs of coordination strain.

**Permitted trigger signals:**

- Hiring for an operations assistant, marketing coordinator, admissions coordinator, or office manager.
- Multiple locations or service areas.
- Active website content, reviews, ads, or lead magnets that imply follow up volume.
- A recent CRM, website, or rebrand investment.
- Publicly visible expansion, new service line, or new location.
- A partner or current client operates in the same market structure without being a direct confidential comparable.

**First actions:**

- Build small lists by vertical, no more than 25 accounts per test batch.
- Research each account enough to name one plausible bottleneck and one reason the timing may be right.
- Offer a diagnostic, not a demo link with no context.
- Test one wedge per batch: lead follow up, CRM workflow QA, meeting action recovery, or content production backlog.
- Stop a batch after the agreed test volume and review reply quality before scaling.

**Owner:** Greta. Mira supports message variants. Maive reviews claims and data use.

**Measure:** Qualified replies, diagnostics booked, cost and time per diagnostic, disqualification rate, and negative replies that indicate a positioning problem.

**Compliance guardrail:** Use business contact data lawfully, honor opt-outs, follow email and messaging rules for each channel, and do not collect private personal data from restricted sources.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Early warning signal | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product readiness is overstated | Demo language says a build is live when it is still a spec or staging preview | Maintain a readiness register with three states only: live, pilot build, and concept. Greta sells from the register. Maive updates it after every verified build | Maive and Greta |
| Heartbeat page is not ready for the pilot start | Build slips while sales materials assume the page is included on day one | Sell the pilot with a written delivery point for the page. Provide roster, board, and digests from day one. Do not discount silently or substitute a static image and call it live | Maive |
| Install orchestrator remains manual longer than expected | Stage times stay flat and Maive becomes the bottleneck | Cap install starts, time every manual step, build the orchestrator in the order of highest repeated effort, and keep concierge installs explicitly labeled internally | Maive |
| Per-client Desk provisioning does not scale | Each install needs custom database or passcode work | Use the workspace model in the current spec until a verified clone path exists. Record every manual provisioning step and treat repeated steps as build requirements | Maive and Wes |
| Claude or Grok seat setup stays partly manual | Verification drills fail or platform instructions drift | Keep guided setup plus drill as the durable control. Version the seat instructions in the Team Pack and retest after platform changes | Maive |
| Quality failure reaches a client customer | QA checklist is rushed, approval rule is unclear, or a workflow edge case was not tested | Use approval gates, independent QA, test contacts, branch tests, and stop paths. Any client-facing incident triggers a same-day review and a Team Pack correction before similar work resumes | Maive and Dex |
| Client data crosses a boundary | A screenshot, lesson, prompt pattern, or report contains identifying detail | Apply the two-layer memory contract and sanitization checklist. Require Maive approval before any generalized lesson enters the Team Pack. Remove and document any leakage the same day it is found | Maive and Lena |

## Launch gate

The service may move from pilot sales to standard sales only when all five conditions are true:

1. Austin has approved final pricing, pilot conversion terms, and the initial management term.
2. The pilot install has produced a timed stage record and a first-value result, or the reason it did not is documented and priced into the next offer.
3. The heartbeat demo state is described accurately in every sales asset.
4. Finn has reviewed pilot delivery cost and set the pricing floor for standard installs.
5. The proposal, data handling summary, approval matrix, and partner economics are ready for use without improvisation.

Until those conditions are met, the correct motion is controlled pilot selling to high-fit accounts, not broad market launch.
