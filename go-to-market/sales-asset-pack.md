## Asset 3: The Ten Minute Demo Script

Format: live screen share. Show, do not describe. The prospect should see real work moving within the first three minutes.

**Minute 0:00 to 1:00. Open on the heartbeat page.**
Show: the team page, lanes lit, work in flight.
Say: "Before anything else, this is what you get. This is a real client team page. Every name is a specialist. The ones lit up are working right now. This page is alive all day, and it is yours to check anytime."
Do not explain how it works underneath. Let the visual land.

**Minute 1:00 to 2:30. Walk the lanes.**
Show: scroll the three lanes, a leader card, two or three specialist cards.
Say: "You talk to one person: your team lead, here at the top. Behind them is the bench. CRM, voice, content, reporting, systems. You never pick who does the work. Your lead routes it, the same way a great operations manager would."

**Minute 2:30 to 4:30. Submit a request, live.**
Show: open the request form and type a real request the prospect mentioned on the way in, or the standing example: "Follow up with every lead from last month who never booked, by text, in our voice."
Say: "Watch how little I have to write. One plain sentence. Now watch what happens on their side." Show the brief appear: objective, steps, verification, definition of done. "I did not write any of that. The team did. That brief is why the work comes back right."

**Minute 4:30 to 6:00. Show the board.**
Show: the board, requests in each status, one flagged as needing the owner.
Say: "Everything you have ever asked for lives here, with its status and its result. This flag is the only kind of interruption you will ever get from us: a decision that is genuinely yours. Everything else, the team handles."

**Minute 6:00 to 7:30. Show a finished deliverable.**
Show: one polished finished piece, rotated by prospect type: a built landing page for a marketer, a voice agent call summary for a sales-driven owner, a weekly report for an operator.
Say: "This is what done looks like. Built, checked against the client's rules, delivered with a summary. Not a draft for you to fix. Finished work."

**Minute 7:30 to 8:30. The memory moment.**
Show: a second request that references the first, or the client memory view if available.
Say: "Here is the part tools cannot copy. The team remembers. Your voice, your offers, your rules, what worked last month. Every request makes the next one faster and better. By month three, owners tell us it feels less like software and more like staff who have worked for them for years."

**Minute 8:30 to 9:15. The two questions.**
Ask, in this order:
1. "If this team was live Monday, what is the first thing you would hand off?"
2. "What is that worth to you, in hours or in deals, if it was simply handled every week?"
Listen. Do not pitch over their answer. Their answer is the pilot scope.

**Minute 9:15 to 10:00. The close.**
Say: "Here is what I recommend. We install your team as a pilot. Your command center live, your tools connected, and that first job you just named, completed on day one. Your effort is fifteen minutes of sign-ins and one short call. If the pilot does not earn its keep, you stop, and you keep everything we built. Shall we pick your install day?"

If they hesitate: "Fair. Take the one-pager. It has the pilot terms in plain language. I will hold a slot until Friday and check in once. If the answer is no, no hard feelings and no sequence of follow-ups."

## Asset 4: Discovery Call Questions

Purpose: surface pain, tools, approval habits, and decision authority through conversation, not interrogation. Ask in order, follow their energy, and let answers run. Twelve questions, each with what it is really measuring noted for the salesperson only.

1. "Walk me through a normal week. Where does your time actually go?" (Pain map, in their words. Listen for recurring operational drag.)
2. "What is the work that only happens when you personally push it?" (Bottleneck identification. This becomes pitch material, verbatim.)
3. "When a new lead comes in, what happens, step by step, and where does it break down?" (Speed-to-lead reality. Almost every service business leaks here.)
4. "What is sitting undone right now that you keep meaning to get to?" (The backlog. Their answer is the pilot scope in embryo.)
5. "What tools are you paying for today, and which ones earn their keep?" (Stack inventory and waste. Sets up the connect-what-you-have position.)
6. "Tell me about the last time a follow-up or a detail slipped and it cost you. What happened?" (Emotional weight and cost of inaction, in a real story.)
7. "If you could clone one employee's judgment, whose would it be, and what would the clone own?" (Reveals what they value and what they would delegate first.)
8. "How do decisions get made on changes like this? Who else weighs in?" (Approval habits and hidden stakeholders, asked early and lightly.)
9. "When you approve work from your team today, what does that look like? Fast, or does it pile up?" (Designs the approval rhythm we promise them.)
10. "What have you tried so far to get this work off your plate: hires, freelancers, software, AI tools?" (History and scar tissue. Their failures define our differentiation.)
11. "If a team had been running this for the last ninety days, what would be different in the business by now?" (Outcome vision, stated by them. Quote it back at the close.)
12. "Assuming it works the way we have described, what would you need to see, and over what period, to make this an easy yes?" (Budget authority and success criteria without ever asking their budget directly. Their answer writes the pilot's definition of done.)

Close the call by playing back their words for questions 4, 6, and 11, then offering the demo or the pilot. Never leave without naming the first job the team would do.

## Asset 5: Objection Handling

Rules for every answer: concede what is true, answer in plain words, never argue, and return to the pilot as the low-risk proof. Honesty is the brand.

**"It is more than I expected on price."**
Response: "That may be true, and I will not pretend otherwise. Compare it to the honest alternative, though: this replaces the loaded cost of the hire you have been trying to make, plus the tools add-ons, plus your own hours at your rate. But do not take my arithmetic. The pilot exists so the numbers prove themselves on your work, in your business, before any real commitment. If it does not pay for itself in the pilot, stopping is the right call, and I will tell you so."

**"We tried AI tools. They did not stick."**
Response: "Almost everyone we talk to has. Tools failed because they handed you homework: another login, another system to feed and manage. This is the opposite shape. There is nothing new for you to run. You ask a person, the team works, finished work comes back. The pilot is ten days of your real requests. Judge it on output, not on my description of it."

**"I am worried about our data."**
Response: "You should be, and I will give you the design, not a promise. Your workspace is private and isolated. Your business facts and customer information are never shared with or used for another client. You connect your own tools with your own sign-ins, we never hold your passwords, and every connection revokes instantly whenever you want. The only thing that improves across clients is general craft: checklists and processes. Never your data. If it helps, we will walk your own IT person or advisor through the setup before you sign anything."

**"My team will resist this."**
Response: "In our experience, staff resist tools that create work for them. This removes work from them. Nobody's job is the inbox triage and data cleanup this team absorbs. We also bring them in during install, show them exactly what the team takes off their plate, and let them hand work off too. The person who resisted hardest usually becomes the heaviest user, because their week visibly improves first."

**"I need to think about it."**
Response: "Of course. Two things that might help the thinking. First, name the one job you would hand off if this were already live. Hold the decision against that job: what does another month of it sitting undone cost? Second, take the pilot terms in writing. There is no long commitment to weigh, only a short proof. I will check in once on Friday, and if the answer is no, that is a complete answer and I will leave it there."
