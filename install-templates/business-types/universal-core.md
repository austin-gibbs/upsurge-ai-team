# The Universal Core

## The statement

This product has no industry limit. It works for any business that takes in work, serves someone, and gets paid, in any field, in any country, at any size. Nothing in the product is built for one industry and stretched to fit others. The core is industry-agnostic by design, and industry appears in exactly one place: the Business Type Pack, which is generated for each client at onboarding.

If a feature, question, or rule only makes sense for one industry, it does not belong in the core. It belongs in that client's pack, or nowhere.

## Where industry is allowed to exist

Industry may appear only in the generated pack layer:

- the words the client sees for a project, a customer, and the main work item
- board stage names and their order
- task categories
- starter requests
- the order of first connections
- the extra questions asked during setup
- what the team looks for first in the client's own materials
- pack-specific guardrails for that business

Everything else runs on the universal core and must work, unchanged, for a bakery, a law office, a boat repair yard, and a business nobody on the team has ever heard of.

## Where industry must never exist

The following parts of the product know nothing about industry. They work from structure, not from trade:

1. **Intake.** A request is a request. Intake captures what the client asked for, who it is for, and when it matters, in whatever words the client used. No intake field assumes an industry.
2. **The board.** The board is stages, work items, owners, and dates. Stage names come from the pack. The board mechanics underneath never change by industry.
3. **Memory.** Client memory stores facts, sources, decisions, and rules. It records what is true about this business. It does not sort the business into an industry box, and no memory template asks for an industry label as a fact that matters.
4. **Routing.** Work is routed by the kind of work it is (writing, numbers, systems, design, research, checking) and by lane strengths. Never by the client's industry. A plumber's report and a lawyer's report route the same way.
5. **Quality checks.** Every deliverable passes the same QA gate: right facts, right words, checked against the live source, in the client's own terms. The gate does not loosen or tighten by industry. Pack guardrails add protection on top; they never replace the gate.
6. **The output ledger.** The ledger records what was delivered, when, and how long it took, from real completion events only. Categories come from the pack. The honesty rules underneath are universal and identical for every client.
7. **The heartbeat and team page.** The team, the lanes, and the pulse are the same for every install. Only the client's names and words change.
8. **Escalation and approvals.** What needs the client's approval, what needs Austin, and what the team may do on standing rules are decided by the kind of action, never by the industry.

## The universal intake questions

These questions work for any business on earth. They are asked at onboarding, in this order, in the client's own setting, and their answers seed the generated pack and the client memory. Ask them in plain words, one at a time, and let the client answer in theirs.

1. **What do you sell?** The offer in their words, including the main thing and anything else people pay them for.
2. **Who buys it?** The customer in their words: who they are, how they find the business, and what makes a good one.
3. **Walk me through one job, from the first time someone contacts you to the moment you get paid.** This is the most important question in onboarding. The answer becomes the board: each step they name is a candidate stage, in their order, in their words.
4. **What do you call your customers, and what do you call the work itself?** The vocabulary seed. Their exact words go into the pack unchanged.
5. **What repeats every week?** The recurring work: the reports, follow ups, checks, and chores that happen on a rhythm. These become the team's standing work candidates.
6. **What do you wish you never had to touch again?** The pain list. These become the first delegation targets and the first starter requests, in the client's own phrasing.

Two follow ups, asked only when the answers leave a gap:

7. **When a job goes wrong, what does that look like, and who hears about it first?** Seeds the guardrails and the escalation preferences.
8. **What does a great month look like, in numbers you already watch?** Seeds the measures the monthly report and the ledger should speak in.

## Rules of the core

1. **The pack is generated, not picked.** A library pack may speed up generation when one happens to fit. The default path for every client is the generator (see pack-generator.md), working from the client's own materials.
2. **Client words beat library words.** When the client's own materials and a library pack disagree on a word, the client's word wins, every time.
3. **Structure is universal, language is local.** The product supplies the skeleton: intake, board, memory, routing, QA, ledger. The pack supplies the skin: words, stages, categories, questions. Never mix the two layers.
4. **No industry field gates anything.** There is no setting, permission, price rule, or feature in the core that turns on or off because of what industry the client is in.
5. **A strange business is a normal business.** If the team cannot classify a business, nothing is wrong. The generator runs on the universal questions and the client's materials, and the pack that comes out is that business's pack.
6. **The core improves through the brain.** When work for an unusual business teaches the team something general, the generalized lesson strengthens the core or the generator through the normal sanitization path. The client's industry detail stays in their layer.
