## Starter requests

- "Make a call list from the recall report I approved for patients due this month."
- "Draft a friendly reminder for tomorrow's visits using the appointment list I approved."
- "Sort these new patient requests and tell me what detail is missing from each one."
- "Write a kind follow up for patients who missed a visit and need to rebook."
- "Turn our approved aftercare sheet into a short, clear patient message."
- "Make a list of treatment follow ups that need a booking reminder."
- "Draft a thank you and review request for a patient whose care is complete."

## Connect first

1. **Practice system.** This is the source for schedules, appointment types, and recall reports. Connect only through the access or reports the practice approves. After sign in or report setup, check appointment types, schedule views, and recall report names. Do not open patient charts unless the practice has first set up access that meets privacy rules.
2. **Phone.** Patients often call for booking, pain, directions, and changes. After sign in, check the office number, call notes, text use, and after hours directions.
3. **Email.** Forms, visit replies, and office messages need a clear home. After sign in, check shared inboxes, saved replies, and who answers each kind of message.
4. **Calendar.** Office hours, provider schedules, and meeting times need to line up with booking. After sign in, check provider calendars, blocked times, reminders, and time zone.

## Questions we ask during setup

- Which services and appointment types do you offer, and how long does each one take?
- When should hygiene recall reminders begin, and how often should they repeat?
- What should happen when a patient misses a visit or cancels late?
- What should a patient with pain or swelling be told to do first?
- Which forms must be finished before a first visit?
- What is the approved way to share schedule and recall reports with the team?

## Extra guardrails

- Pack safety rule: The team works around the practice system, never inside patient records unless the client arranges compliant access.
- Use the least patient detail needed for the task. Prefer approved reports and lists over opening a chart.
- Never copy diagnoses, treatment notes, images, or private health details into a tool the practice has not approved for that use.
- Only the practice's licensed care team may give dental advice, diagnose, change a treatment plan, or judge how urgent a health concern is.
- If a patient describes a medical emergency, use the practice's approved emergency directions.
- Do not promise that insurance will pay, that a treatment will work, or that a visit time is open until the practice confirms it.

## Client choices

You stay in control of the words and the flow. You can:

- call a treatment plan, patient, appointment, or recall by a different name
- rename, add, remove, or reorder any board stage
- add or remove any work group
- save your own favorite starter requests
- choose a different tool to connect first
- tell the team to stop using any word that does not sound like your practice
