## Words this business uses

| General idea | Words to use | What it means here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project | Job | All the steps, people, times, notes, photos, and payments tied to one piece of work. |
| Customer | Customer | The person or business that asked for the work and will approve or pay for it. |
| Main work item | Service call | A visit to check a problem, give an estimate, do the work, or return for a follow up. |
| New person | New request | Someone who asked for service but has not yet booked or approved a job. |
| Price step | Estimate | A written scope and price for the client to approve, prepared under the client's rules. |
| Work team | Crew | The people assigned to do the work at the customer's place. |
| Done | Finished and paid | The work is complete, notes and photos are saved, the customer knows the result, and payment is complete or the next payment step is clear. |

## Starter requests

- "Call or message every new service request from today and list anyone who still needs a reply."
- "Make a reminder list for estimates that were sent but not approved."
- "Build tomorrow's crew plan with job addresses, start times, and customer names."
- "Check today's jobs and tell me which ones are missing notes, photos, or payment."
- "Draft a friendly update for customers whose crew is running late."
- "Make a list of finished jobs that need a review request."
- "Find repeat service customers who are due for another visit."
- "Write a weekly summary of new requests, estimates, finished jobs, and unpaid work."

## Connect first

1. **Field service system or CRM.** Jobs, customers, estimates, crews, notes, and job status need one home. After sign in, check job steps, crew names, customer tags, estimate states, and who owns each job.
2. **Phone.** New requests, urgent problems, crew updates, and customer questions often start here. After sign in, check the business number, call notes, text use, and after hours directions.
3. **Email.** Estimates, invoices, photos, supplier notes, and customer replies need a clear record. After sign in, check shared inboxes, saved replies, and who answers each kind of message.
4. **Calendar.** Estimates, service calls, crew days, and follow up visits need one time view. After sign in, check crew calendars, appointment types, travel blocks, reminders, and time zone.
5. **Accounting.** Deposits, invoices, and paid or unpaid status must be clear without exposing bank details. After sign in, check invoice states, payment terms, and who handles money questions.

## Questions we ask during setup

- Which services do you offer, and which areas do you serve?
- Which requests count as urgent, and what should the customer be told first?
- What are the steps from a new request to an approved estimate?
- How are crews named, assigned, and updated during the day?
- When is a deposit required, and who approves price or scope changes?
- What warranty or follow up promise do you make after a job?
- Which photos, notes, or checks must be saved before a job can be marked finished?

## Extra guardrails

- Do not promise an arrival time, price, repair result, or warranty term until the client approves it.
- Only the client or their approved lead may change a price, scope, or schedule promise.
- If a request sounds dangerous, follow the client's safety words first. For fire, gas danger, flooding with live power, or another emergency, the customer should be directed to the proper emergency or utility help right away.
- Keep gate codes, alarm details, keys, and access notes only in the tools the client approves for that information.
- Do not mark a job finished until the client's required notes, photos, and payment state have been checked.

## Client choices

You stay in control of the words and the flow. You can:

- call a job, customer, service call, estimate, or crew by a different name
- rename, add, remove, or reorder any board stage
- add or remove any work group
- save your own favorite starter requests
- choose a different tool to connect first
- tell the team to stop using any word that does not sound like your company
